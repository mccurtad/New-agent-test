# Worked Example: Module 1 Discussion

Illustrates the [discussion design pattern](../../references/discussion-design.md):
splitting a combined icebreaker + content prompt into two separate, correctly
placed activities.

## Original prompt (as received)

The instructor's original discussion combined a personal introduction with a
business-case framing exercise, and was tagged to the wrong module in the
course map (Module 2, even though it was intended for Week 1 and its content
only maps to Module 1 objectives).

## Split 1 — Orientation: Introduce Yourself

Pure icebreaker, no MLO attached, lives in Orientation.

**Initial post (~150–200 words):**
> Welcome! Tell your classmates a little about yourself:
> - Your name and the program you're in — and roughly where you are in it
> - Why you're taking this course and what you're hoping to get out of it
> - Have you used this LMS before?
> - Outside of school, what keeps you busy — do you work, and if so, in what field?
> - How much do you already know about that field, and what draws you to it?

**Peer response:** Reply to at least one classmate — say hello and mention
something you have in common with them or find interesting about their
background.

**Grading:** Complete/Incomplete (posted + replied).

## Split 2 — Module 1: From Business Problem to Machine Learning Task

Content-only, tied to MLOs 1.01–1.04, lives in the actual module.

**Initial post (~175–200 words):**
> Post a 175–200 word response directly in the discussion forum. Choose a
> realistic business problem from an industry you know or are interested in,
> and address:
>
> 1. What business problem is the organization trying to solve?
> 2. What would you want a machine-learning model to predict or discover?
> 3. Would this most likely be supervised or unsupervised learning? If
>    supervised, would it be regression or classification?
> 4. How could the organization use the result to make a better business
>    decision?
> 5. In 1–2 sentences: before this course, had you thought about the
>    difference between a system that follows programmed rules and one that
>    learns from data? Which step of the machine learning workflow are you
>    most curious to dig into, and why?

**Peer response (~50–75 words):** Respond to one classmate by identifying one
strength in their framing and one question or suggestion that could make the
business use of the analysis clearer.

**Grading — Complete/Incomplete version:**

| Criterion | Complete if... |
|---|---|
| Initial post | Addresses the prompt with a real example (not copied from lecture), roughly the target length |
| Peer reply | Responds with a specific comment or question, not just "great post!" |

**Grading — point-rubric version** (if you want differentiation instead):

| Criterion | 2 pts | 1 pt | 0 pts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Example correctly reflects the concept | Mostly correct, minor confusion | Misunderstands the concept |
| Specificity | Concrete, original example | Vague or generic example | No real example given |
| Peer engagement | Reply adds a question or specific insight | Reply is generic but on-topic | No reply / off-topic |

Question 5 was added specifically to close a coverage gap — see
[`course-map.md`](course-map.md) for the full MLO-to-assessment mapping this
was built to satisfy.
