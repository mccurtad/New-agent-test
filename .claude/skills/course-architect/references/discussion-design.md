# Discussion & Rubric Design

A lightweight pattern for discussion boards that (a) give genuine
constructed-response evidence for "Explain"/"Describe"-type objectives,
(b) create real student-to-student connection, and (c) don't cost the
instructor much grading time.

## The core pattern

**Initial post (short — ~150–250 words):** a prompt tied directly to the
module's content, ideally asking the student to apply a concept to an example
of their own choosing rather than recite lecture material back. A self-chosen
example is harder to plagiarize/generate thoughtlessly and gives a better
signal of understanding.

**Peer reply (short — ~30–75 words):** every student replies to at least one
classmate, naming one specific strength and asking one question or suggestion.
"Respond to a classmate" alone invites "great post!" — require a *specific*
strength and a *specific* question/suggestion to get a real exchange instead.

This peer-reply requirement is what supplies student-to-student connection —
it works whether the initial prompt is a pure icebreaker or fully
content-focused, so you don't need a separate "connection" activity layered
on top.

## Keep icebreaker and content-aligned discussions separate

Don't combine "introduce yourself" with a content-graded prompt in the same
post. They serve different purposes and belong in different places:

- **Icebreaker / intro discussion** → belongs in Orientation (or Week 0), not
  inside a content module. No MLO attached; grade Complete/Incomplete if
  graded at all.
- **Content discussion** → belongs in the module whose MLO(s) it's actually
  evidence for, with nothing but the content prompt and peer-reply
  instructions.

If an existing discussion mixes both, split it: move the personal-intro
questions to Orientation, and keep only the content questions (plus the peer
reply) in the module discussion.

## Rubric options, cheapest first

**Complete/Incomplete** (lowest effort): Canvas supports this natively for
discussions. Define 2 criteria as plain checks — e.g. "Initial post addresses
the prompt with a real, original example" and "Peer reply gives a specific
comment or question" — and grade with a single click per student.

**Simple point rubric** (a bit more differentiation): 3 criteria, 2 points
each, e.g.

| Criterion | 2 pts | 1 pt | 0 pts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Correctly reflects the concept | Mostly correct, minor confusion | Misunderstands the concept |
| Specificity | Concrete, original example | Vague/generic example | No real example |
| Peer engagement | Specific question or insight | Generic but on-topic | No reply / off-topic |

Pick whichever matches how much differentiation the grading weight actually
warrants — a discussion worth a small slice of a "participation" bucket
usually doesn't need more than Complete/Incomplete.
